# Product Vision

## Meaning

**Status: Provisional.** xkcd Reading Tracker helps a reader keep their own place in xkcd: resume naturally, remember favorites and reactions, and explore the archive without replacing the comic's own reading surface. This Vision preserves the intended reader experience and product character while its exact wording remains open for Wolf's ratification.

## The Experience

Reading xkcd over months or years should feel continuous rather than administrative. A reader should be able to answer simple personal questions—where did I stop, have I read this, which comics mattered to me, what remains—without maintaining a separate spreadsheet or surrendering the experience to a new application.

The extension should feel like xkcd acquired a small memory. Its controls belong beside the comic, use restrained language and presentation, and leave the site's original behavior available.

## What Matters

- **A trustworthy place to resume.** Read state and the continue point remain distinct so rereading one comic does not move a backlog position.
- **Personal meaning without forced taxonomy.** Favorites and ratings are independent of read state and optional to use.
- **Useful archive exploration.** Unread and favorite browsing, ranges, statistics, and search make a long archive tractable.
- **Ownership of reading data.** State is portable through backups, understandable, and protected from avoidable loss.
- **Quiet integration.** The extension augments xkcd rather than becoming a generic dashboard, social product, or content mirror.
- **Privacy by smallness.** No developer account, backend, analytics, telemetry, advertising, or reading-history service is needed for the product to work.

## Product Character

The visual language should remain modest, legible, and a little playful: ordinary links and buttons, xkcd-compatible typography, clear state, and no decorative application chrome competing with the comic.

The implementation should remain understandable enough that a maintainer can change it without operating a service or framework merely to preserve a reading list.

## Boundaries

The Vision does not require one permanent browser, storage provider, or feature set. It does reject complexity whose only justification is a hypothetical future product. Accounts, external synchronization, cross-browser support, richer personal annotations, and new integrations become candidates only when a concrete reader need earns their semantics and maintenance cost.
